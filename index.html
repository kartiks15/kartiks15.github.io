Research

How green is compression? The energy cost of 4-bit LLMs

Oct 2026

B.Tech capstone · team of three, mentored by Prof. Rajesh Verma

4-bit quantization makes language models cheaper to deploy, and it is often assumed to make them greener too. We measured it. On an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU we compared three 4-bit formats with the 16-bit baseline in five open models: FP4 and NF4 weight-only quantization, run by Triton kernels we wrote, and NVIDIA's native NVFP4, which does the arithmetic itself in 4 bits on the GPU's FP4 tensor cores.

Models: Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-3-mini and Gemma-3-4B, scored on all 14,042 MMLU questions and WikiText-2 perplexity. Energy came from the GPU's own counter with the idle floor removed, repeated in Williams-ordered cycles with 95% intervals, and accuracy differences were checked with Holm-corrected McNemar tests.

  • Generating text, every 4-bit format cut energy per token to 44–57% of the 16-bit baseline in all five models, mostly by drawing less power (340 W down to 190 W on Mistral-7B).
  • Batched work is different. FP4 and NF4 used 3–8% more energy than the baseline over a full MMLU run. Only NVFP4, whose arithmetic runs in 4 bits, saved energy there: 50–53% of the baseline, 71% on Gemma-3-4B.
  • NF4 is the better weight-only format: about 12% less weight error than FP4, and never significantly less accurate.
  • The cheapest format isn't always safe. NVFP4 lost 2.7 points on Qwen2.5-7B and 4.7 on Phi-3-mini against the best weight-only format. Energy effects held in every model; accuracy effects did not.

Next: test a production INT4 kernel (Marlin), fuse NVFP4's activation quantization to cut its overhead, and extend the study to 70B-class models.

SafeSite Finalist

Nov 2026

Near-miss and hazard reporting for a chemical plant. A worker reports with a photo and a voice note in English, Hindi, Marathi or Hinglish, even with no signal. An AI model drafts the description and labels, and code checks the draft against the worker's own words. My part: I built a complete second version overnight. Its plant map, worker PIN sign-in, offline reporting, photo privacy (location data stripped, faces blurred), on-server voice-to-text, induction quiz and Docker deployment were merged into the team's final app.

Baadhi Watch

2026

Maps flood damage from satellite data. Given an area and a date, it fuses Sentinel-1 radar, Sentinel-2 optical imagery and elevation with pre-flood OpenStreetMap to show where the water went, which buildings, roads and bridges were hit and which settlements are cut off from hospitals, then writes a situation report in English and Nepali. On the August 2026 Nepal flood it matched Copernicus EMS damage maps with 0.71 IoU (F1 0.83), found 96% of damaged buildings and ran in 526 seconds from raw data. Built for Track B of the Multimodal AI Hackathon 2026 on Devpost.
